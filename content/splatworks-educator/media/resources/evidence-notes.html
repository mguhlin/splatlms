Instructional evidence and effect-size notes

The course combines well-supported instructional approaches, including several with substantial average research effects. Estimates describe research syntheses; they are not measured effects of any SplatWorks app. Comparison conditions, measures, populations and research designs differ. Do not add the estimates or convert them to percentage gains.

Teacher clarity — Visible Learning MetaX, February 2026

The current MetaX entry reports a weighted mean effect size of 0.85 for teacher clarity. Its underlying syntheses concern relationships between clarity and learning; the figure is not a forecast of gains from a particular app.

Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie (2020), The Power of Feedback Revisited

This meta-analysis reports d = 0.48 after excluding extreme values. A high-information-feedback subgroup reports d = 0.99. The subgroup is not interchangeable with the overall estimate, and effects vary widely.

Read the primary study or synthesis

Yang et al. (2021), Testing (Quizzing) Boosts Classroom Learning

A review of 222 classroom studies reports an overall Hedges’ g of 0.499 for quizzing. Comparators, timing and feedback matter; typing a displayed answer is not the same task as recalling it.

Read the primary study or synthesis

Bisra et al. (2018), Inducing Self-Explanation: A Meta-Analysis

The study reports an overall weighted mean g = 0.55 for prompted self-explanation. Ask learners to explain why a step or relationship makes sense, rather than simply narrating clicks.

EEF, Metacognition and self-regulation

EEF summarizes this strand as high impact, averaging eight additional months of progress. Months of progress are a separate metric from d or g. Teach planning, monitoring and evaluation within subject learning.

Read the primary study or synthesis

EEF, Collaborative learning approaches

EEF reports five additional months on average, with limited evidence security. Structure participation and a shared goal; placing students at the same screen does not by itself create effective collaboration.

Using the evidence

Make a content goal explicit, model the important reasoning, elicit a response from every learner, give feedback tied to a criterion, allow a revision and check a new example later. These are the course’s original design applications. A tool only helps when the task actually calls for the strategy. Use classroom evidence to evaluate the fit in your context.

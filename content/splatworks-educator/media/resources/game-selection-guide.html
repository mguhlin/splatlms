K–12 game selection and transfer guide

These grade bands are course recommendations for teacher preview, not formal content ratings or guarantees of suitability.

Starting pointOptions to previewInstructional follow-up
Early learners / K–2 readinessPaws & Keys (stated design ages 4–9), a short adult-supported sketch or simple tangram task.Name a key or strategy, practice briefly and explain one choice. Offer pointing or an off-screen equivalent.
Elementary / grades 3–5 readinessShort Story Sprint passages, everyday-word Wordfall, Tangram, simple Flow Free or a teacher-created bingo activity.Focus on personal accuracy or a strategy. Check word meaning or subject understanding with a new response.
Middle grades / grades 6–8 readinessSubject passages or vocabulary, SplatBot, Lights Out, Untangle, Dots and Boxes.Predict, test, debug and explain; move to a new problem without the game cues.
Secondary / grades 9–12 readinessLonger passage practice, strategy/puzzle challenges, or a reviewed older-learner strategy game.Explain a strategy, analyze an error pattern and evaluate transfer to the target content.

Preview before assigning

  • Readability and vocabulary; whether the learner can use the controls.
  • Sound, motion, timing, color dependence and access with assistive technology.
  • Conflict/traffic themes in Castles & Catapults, Super Star Trek, Cipher Chase or Squirrel Run; trivia content in Fun Quiz.
  • A private personal goal, a pause/stop routine and an alternative without public speed comparison.
  • A short purpose-driven interval, typically three to five minutes before a debrief.

Station-card prompts

Purpose; controls; practice interval; personal goal; what to monitor; equivalent alternative; debrief; fresh transfer task. Suggested debrief: What did you predict, what happened, and what will you change?

The all-tools field guide links every game. Cipher Chase is the car-and-typing experience; Story Sprint is passage practice. Concept artwork may depict them differently.

Walkthrough: a one-minute evidence explainer

Open the captioned video · WebVTT captions

Storyboard the reasoning

  • Question and claim
  • Chart and evidence
  • Limitation and next observation

Plan a forty-five to seventy-five second explanation. Use three moments: the question and claim, the chart and evidence, and a limitation with a next observation. Draft about one hundred to one hundred forty words. Use the supplied fictional data or a non-sensitive classroom dataset, and label it honestly. Keep the chart’s unit labels visible.

Build and revise

  • Record or use slides with notes
  • Trim unnecessary material
  • Preserve the source project

Choose a recording or slide-and-text route. Record narration in AudioSplat if helpful, then assemble the explanation in ClipSplat or VideoSplat. Use MediaSplat for a simple trim or join. Remove material that does not help the audience follow the reasoning. Keep the editable project so you can revise after feedback rather than starting again from the exported video.

Test what the audience can learn

  • Verify the values
  • Check without sound
  • Name the remaining uncertainty

Ask a partner to state your claim, verify the two values and name the limitation. Test the explanation without sound. Correct captions and provide the transcript. Your deliverable is the explanation, source, chart and access check. If a partner cannot explain the reasoning after viewing it, revise the content before adjusting the colors or adding more effects.

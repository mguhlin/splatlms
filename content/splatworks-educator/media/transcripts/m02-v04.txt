Walkthrough: build an explanation board

Choose a misconception
Choose one misconception from a unit you teach. Our example is the assumption that more sunlight must always be better for every plant. Select observations that make the decision less automatic. Create a model panel that names the claim, two pieces of evidence and a limitation. A clear small task is better than a board that tries to teach an entire unit at once.

Build the guided and independent cases
Build the partly completed example beside the model. Remove a reasoning step and leave a question that invites explanation. Create an independent case with different observations. Add one purposeful asset and its text equivalent. Decide how learners will respond by drawing, writing or speaking, using the same content criteria across response modes.

Rehearse and recover
Rehearse your think-aloud in about a minute. Ask a colleague to find the claim and evidence without your help. Check whether the role rotation gives each learner a chance to reason. Save and reopen the editable board. Your practice deliverable is the three-panel sequence, one visual asset and a short modeling script. Revise the instructions if a colleague cannot tell what students are meant to learn.
